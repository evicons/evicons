## Norm check: `plug-ccs2`

The IEC 62196-3 standard itself was **not** consulted (it is paywalled). The layout was checked against two public drawings on Wikimedia Commons, retrieved 2026-10-21:

1. `File:IEC 62196 Type 2 (M, DC, CCS Combo 2).svg` by Mliu92 (CC BY-SA 4.0), "Male plug for CCS Combo 2, based on IEC 62196-2 Type 2 extended with high voltage DC connectors". https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:IEC_62196_Type_2_(M,_DC,_CCS_Combo_2).svg
2. `File:Combo-ccs-eu.svg` by GreatDrok (CC BY-SA 4.0), "Type 2 CCS (Europe) slow AC and fast DC connector". https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Combo-ccs-eu.svg

Values measured from the drawings' SVG source (drawing units):

- Drawing 1: Type 2 outline R 260, flat top 191 above the PE centre (0.735 R); contact pitch 160, power contacts r 70, signal contacts r 50; top row 112 (0.70 pitch) above and bottom row 139 (0.869 pitch) below the middle row. DC part: a separate stadium (rounded rectangle with fully round ends) 565 × 295, 87.5 below the Type 2 outline; DC+ and DC− r 115, concentric with the stadium's round ends, 135 left and right of the centre line.
- Drawing 2: Type 2 pitch about 103 with the same row arrangement; DC part a stadium 389 × 192, about as wide as the Type 2 part; DC contacts r about 53, 86 left and right of the centre line.

Both drawings agree on the arrangement: Type 2 face on top with all seven contacts, a separate DC opening below it with two large contacts side by side. In drawing 1 the AC contacts L1–L3 and N are shown greyed (unused by the DC cable connector); the vehicle inlet has all seven.

Smallest gaps (centre lines minus radii): signal contact to flat edge 2.015, signal to power contact 2.019, power to power 2.30 (row) and 2.31 (diagonal), power contact to outline 2.06 (bottom row) and 2.10 (L1/N), DC contact to stadium 2.025 all round, Type 2 outline to DC stadium 2.0. All at or above the connector minimum of 2.0.

Deviations, deliberate:

- **Scaled-down Type 2 part.** The two parts must fit into the live area one above the other (total height 20.175 of 20.25 available centre-line units), so the Type 2 part has R 6.65 instead of 9 (`plug-type-2`). Scaling the `plug-type-2` contacts with it (r 0.92 / 0.74, pitch 3.92) would put neighbouring contacts 1.71 apart, below the minimum of 2.0. The contacts were therefore shrunk more than the outline: power r 0.75, signal r 0.5. Both are below half the stroke, so all seven Type 2 contacts render as **dots**, not rings. This departs from owner decision O2 for `plug-type-2` (signal contacts r 1.0 as rings); at this size it is not possible within the 2.0 minimum. Power and signal contacts are still drawn in different sizes.
- Contacts are drawn smaller relative to their housings than in the drawings (as in `plug-type-2`), so the 1.5 strokes do not merge; for the DC contacts the ratio contact/half stadium height is 0.36 (drawing 1: 0.78).
- Left/right labelling: drawing 1 labels CP left, PP right, N left, L1 right, DC+ left, DC− right although it is titled "male"; this is the same labelling as the female Type 2 end view by the same author, so the title or the labels are mirrored. The icon has no labels and its contact layout is left/right symmetric in size, so this does not change the geometry. The Type 2 part follows the `plug-type-2` convention (male inlet, seen from the front).

Open: a check against the actual standard sheet of IEC 62196-3 (vehicle inlet, configuration FF) is still outstanding.
